Intended use and limitations
===========================

Intended use
------------
This artifact is the research prototype accompanying the paper, intended for
reproducing our measurements and for studying how cryptographic per-query
consent composes with FHE-backed encrypted databases; it is not a deployable
clinical system.

Scope
-----
The prototype runs the discovery protocol end to end across six entities: a
user, a privacy proxy, a key manager, data holders, evaluators and patients.
Each provider evaluates predicates homomorphically, every enrolled patient
blinds its own rows, and only a fresh per-query patient signature lets the proxy
cancel that blinding. We include five interchangeable backends, a plaintext
baseline and four FHE engines.

Limitations
-----------
By default the entities run as separate services on one host, so co-located
providers contend for CPU.

Quantized columns are capped at 8 bits. The indicator polynomial has degree
equal to the column domain and coefficients bounded by the plaintext modulus,
so under the shipped parameters an 8-bit domain is exact, a 9-bit domain passes
some runs and not others, and 10 bits and above decrypt to noise.

Per-patient consent costs one cancellation ciphertext of about 1 MiB at the
Lattigo parameter set, so a cohort of a million patients demands roughly a
terabyte of proxy-side storage; the paper therefore caps the consenting cohort
at the two largest record counts.

The approximate CKKS comparator in the PatDiscover backend resolves differences
no finer than about 0.008 in its normalized band, admitting rows within roughly
two quantization levels of a threshold on 8-bit columns, and reports around
99 percent accuracy where the other backends are exact.

HE3DB cannot hold the per-provider evaluation keys for large federations; the
paper reports it up to 25 providers.


Data
----
The artifact contains no patient data; README.txt describes
record synthesis and the requirements for substituting real cohorts.

Security
--------
The prototype is not hardened:  Do not point it
at real patient data outside a controlled environment.
